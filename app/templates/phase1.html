Phase 1

Business & Data Understanding

What problem is being solved, for whom, what the model must answer, which data exists, whether it is good enough, and how success will be measured — fixed before training anything.

1.1 The approved problem and its context

UMBRAL is a game about grief: Eco, the hero, crosses five worlds (sadness, denial, anger, bargaining, depression) and fights a boss in each one, then the Wolf at the Threshold. The design goal of the team is that bosses feel like they learn you: a player who always shields gets feinted, one who always dodges backwards gets aimed at, one who repeats a pattern gets read. The game already does this with three small models that learn online while you play (scripts/ml_brain.gd): an order-2 Markov chain that predicts your next action, a running mean of how you dodge, and a contextual bandit (one logistic regression per attack) that estimates whether an attack will hit you.

Approved project: study the whole ML of the game with CRISP-ML — measure how well those three models really work, compare them with other algorithms on recorded play data, choose the best one with a criterion fixed in advance, deploy it, and plan its monitoring.

Questions the model answers

  1. Given the player's last actions and the situation, which action comes next, and how sure are we?
  2. If the boss uses attack m now, what is the probability that it hits?
  3. Where will this player move while the boss announces a charge?

1.4 Quality, relevance and sufficiency

  • Quality: the log is written by the game itself, so values are typed and bounded; known problems are measured in Phase 2: duplicated batches ({{ b.duplicate_events }} events), distance "9999" when no enemy is found ({{ b.ne_sentinel_9999 }}), long pauses.
  • Relevance: it records exactly what the in-game models see at the moment they decide, so a better model can be swapped in without new sensors.
  • Sufficiency: the minimum to train on real data was fixed at {{ '{:,}'.format(D.real_available.min_actions) }} actions from {{ D.real_available.min_players }} players, and at least 10 players to split by player. Until then the pipeline runs on simulated players and says so on every page.
